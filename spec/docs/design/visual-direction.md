# Visual direction: Arcade

Chosen by the user on 2026-11-17 after comparing five directions. This is the look and feel for the whole application. Where it conflicts with visual details in `wireframes.md`, this document wins; the wireframes still govern what goes on each screen.

- Mock-up canvas (private to the user): https://claude.ai/artifact/6WaH2Rjr5XcBLw4TZjfPX2, artboard "E · Arcade".
- Mock-up source: `reference/arcade-mockup.dc.html`. It is written in a design-canvas component format (`<x-dc>`, `<sc-for>`, `{{holes}}`), so it does not run on its own. Use it as the exact reference for markup structure, sizes and the animation logic, and port it to plain HTML, CSS and ES modules.

## The idea

The feel of an early two-player arcade cabinet, inspired by Pong without copying it: a black field, white blocks, chunky pixel lettering, a thick dashed line as the divider. Two strategies facing each other across a centre line is the core image, which suits head-to-head game-theory worlds. Do not build a playable ball-and-paddle game or reuse that game's name or assets.

## Tokens

| Token | Value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `--bg` | `#000000` | Page ground |
| `--fg` | `#FFFFFF` | Text, blocks, borders |
| `--muted` | `#A6A6A6` | Secondary text, labels, table headers |
| `--ghost` | `#2E2E2E` | Outline of squares not yet played |
| `--font-display` | `'Press Start 2P', monospace` | Logo, nav, headings, scores, ranks, buttons |
| `--font-body` | `'IBM Plex Mono', ui-monospace, monospace` | Everything else (14px, weights 400 and 600) |

- No gradients, blurred shadows or rounded corners. Two signal colours only, defined as tokens in `style.css`: blue `--coop` (#3fc5f0) = cooperate, magenta `--defect` (#e25bd8) = defect. Neither colour may read as a verdict (no red, no green, no slash): defecting is a move, not a mistake. They mark moves and are never used for decoration, body text or buttons; everything else stays black, white and grey. A move square is always filled, so that an empty-looking square cannot be read as "no points". If a later world needs more than two categories, ask the user before adding a colour.
- **Flag colours** (user, 2026-11-22): red `--alert` (#ff4d4d) and yellow `--caution` (#ffd23f). Unlike the signal colours they are a verdict on purpose, and they are used on flags only (frame, mark and title), never on moves, buttons or body text. See the Flags component.
- **Winners are never coloured:** the square that won a round gets a white outline (its border turns white). Whoever leads on the total or won a match is raised: nudged up and left over a hard-edged block shadow (`--raise`), so it stands out from the field. A leading total is also shown as a white block with a text tag ("Leads", "Wins"). The shadow is never blurred.
- Borders: 3px on small squares and bars, 4px on buttons, 6px dashed under the header, 8px dashed for the centre line.
- Display type sizes: logo 18px, nav and buttons 11px, section headings 13px, ranks and scores 12px, match scores `clamp(36px, 8vw, 88px)`. All display text is upper case. Never set body copy in the display font.
- Fonts: both are open-licensed (SIL OFL). Self-host the woff2 files under `public/fonts/` rather than linking to Google Fonts, so the app works offline in its containers.

## Components

- **Header:** logo, nav links, action buttons, with the 6px dashed rule beneath. The active nav link is inverted (white block, black text). Primary button is a solid white block with black text; secondary is black with a 4px white border. Minimum control height 44px.
- **Match view:** shown on a result only once a match is picked from the head-to-head table, under a heading that names the two sides. Two halves split by the dashed centre line. Player name in display type above a huge score; left side right-aligned, right side left-aligned, so both hug the line.
- **Round strip:** one column per round, top square for the left player and bottom for the right. Blue square = cooperate, magenta square = defect, ghost outline = not yet played. Squares are 18px to 28px wide; rounds that do not fit on one line carry on on the next, so the strip never scrolls sideways. Always shown with its legend.
- **The race (top of a result):** the run told as a league season (`components/race.js`). Every match day each competitor plays one whole match against one opponent; with an odd field one sits on the bench. The order of the matches is drawn from the run's seed. A line chart shows total points after each match day, one line per competitor: grey, 2px, the leader white; each ends in a block with the competitor's avatar, name and total beside it, so lines are told apart without colour. Faint grid lines mark the match days.
- **Fixtures board:** while the race plays, a bordered box above the chart lists the matches of the match day on show, football style: home name and avatar, the two scores in display type meeting in the middle, away avatar and name, and a line for who is on the bench. At the whistle the side with more points turns white and is underlined. The board is wiped (a hard-stepped wipe to the right) before the next match day and is gone when the season is over.
- **High scores (leaderboard):** rank, name, a 20px-high bar, total points. Bar width is the total as a share of the maximum. Bars are white: they show the score and nothing else (user, 2026-11-22).
- **Head to head (pairwise matrix):** sits under the high scores and shows the row's average points per round against the column. The cells share the width of the box and the lettering shrinks with them, so it never scrolls sideways; cells are 44px high, lower when a big field would otherwise not fit the window. Cell background is a grey from `rgb(30,30,30)` (lowest score) to white (highest); text flips to black once the grey is lighter than 140.
- **Section headings:** display font, 13px, upper case, no decoration.
- **Working:** while rules are being written from a description, a bordered box shows a row of eight blocks with a lit pair marching along it, "Working on your rules" in display type with a blinking caret, and one line on what is happening. Still under reduced motion.
- **Flags:** a box that needs the author's attention before they go on, used where a plain-English description is turned into rules. A 4px solid frame in the flag colour, a 32px solid block in that colour carrying a black mark, and a title in display type in the same colour; the details beneath are ordinary body text. Two kinds, each with its own box, the red one first: "Violates the rules" (red, mark X) when the description asks for something no strategy may do, and "Made assumptions" (yellow, mark !) when a choice was made for the author. The mark and the title carry the meaning without the colour.

- **Avatars:** every strategy has a pixel figure, 8 × 8 white blocks (`components/avatar.js`). The four built-in classics are hand-drawn; every other strategy's figure is drawn from its id, so it is the same on every screen and nothing is stored. Show the avatar wherever a strategy is named (cards, run form, match view, high scores, head to head, world page, runs list), at a multiple of 8px, always next to the name. A strategy that was just created is shown off on its own screen before anything else ("Your new competitor"): the avatar, 192px, is built block by block on a dashed floor line, then turns on the spot in pixel steps (it narrows to a sliver and widens again, twice, with a small hop, then stands still for a moment), with its name in display type beneath.
- **Front page:** play before explanation, one idea per step (`pages/landing.js`): a hero with one promise and one action; five rounds played by hand against an opponent that has a face and talks in a speech bubble; the opponent then names itself and its rule (what a strategy is); strategies play each other and are ranked; three steps and one closing action. Explanations in depth live on `/how-it-works`. An opponent's speech bubble is a bordered box with a blocky tail towards its avatar, and reacts to what the visitor just did. Each step fills the window (`.landing-screen`); the hero shows one button and nothing of the next step. In the hand-played game each round shows the points both sides won, with the round's winner outlined in white, and the total of whoever leads raised and tagged. Squares large enough carry the letter of their move (C, D). The two sides are told apart by place, not colour: the visitor's lane and the panel with their move buttons sit on a grey band (`--lane`) with a white edge and a white "You" tag; the opponent's lane is plain, with its avatar and name. The two move buttons look the same, so neither reads as the right choice.
- **Films (How it works page):** the pictures that explain something are short films (`components/film.js`, chapters in `pages/how-it-works.js`): a framed screen with a chapter bar on top, the picture in the middle and a caption beneath, next to the list of chapters. Chapters play one after another and each is narrated by captions. A film starts by itself the first time most of its screen is in view, stops where it is when scrolled away and carries on when it is back; it plays once, then offers Replay. Pause and the chapter list (jump to a chapter) are always available. A playing film must look like it is running: a blinking light and PLAYING in its top bar, a progress bar that fills chapter by chapter, a title card before each chapter, captions typed out behind a blinking caret, and avatars that hop on the spot. All of it stops when the film is paused, and the picture dims. Films use only the parts above: avatars, move squares, the centre line, bars, pixel lettering.
- **How it works page, one screen per section:** like the front page, every section fills the window and fits inside it (`.how-screen`): three films and a closing screen. The film's picture is sized from the window's height (300px at least, 440px at most), only the chapter on show keeps its description, and anything a film points at (the points table) sits under its chapter list, not in a section of its own. Below 900px width the film and its chapters stack, and a section may be taller than the window.
- **Magnetic screens:** on every page the scroll snaps to the screens (CSS scroll snap on `html:has(.landing)` and `html:has(.screens)`): the transition between two screens is visible while scrolling, and letting go settles on one of them. Mandatory from 901px width, where every screen fits the window; only a gentle pull below that. Every section of the front page and How it works has an `id`, so it can be linked (`/#ranking`, `/how-it-works#prisoners-dilemma`); the router scrolls to it.
- **One thing per screen, on every page (user, 2026-11-22):** every page is a column of screens (`components/screen.js`, `.screens` > `.screen`). A screen is as tall as the window (the first one: the window minus the header), holds one thing to look at, centred, and must fit the window at 1280 × 720 and up; nothing may be cut off by the bottom edge. A page that does not build screens itself is given a single one by the router. What goes on a screen is sized to it rather than left to overflow: long lists are paged to the window's height (`components/pager.js`: runs, strategies, a world's high scores), the race chart takes the height its screen has left, head-to-head rows get lower for a big field, folding cards in one list open one at a time, and long reading (a world's story) sits behind a fold. Where saving or starting is a screen further down, the screen before ends in a "Next: …" button. A screen must not be mostly empty on a laptop either (user, 2026-11-22): from 901px width, sections that would each leave the window bare share one screen side by side (`screenGroup`); narrower, each is a screen of its own again. Screens per page on a laptop: a world = what it is with its rules and points, then high scores beside indicators with the baseline actors underneath; a run = the race, then high scores beside indicators and downloads, then head to head beside the match picked from it (a prompt until one is picked); strategies = the introduction with the first group, then the other two groups side by side; new run = who plays beside the settings; the strategy editor = writing it, then preview and save. A screen may still grow past the window when the visitor opens something large (rules built by hand) or at phone width; it can then be scrolled through.
- **Folding cards (strategies, worlds):** lists of strategies and worlds use `components/fold.js`, a native `<details>` in a bordered card. Folded, a card is one line: a +/- marker, the avatar and name, and its tags on the right, so many fit on a screen. Folded out, it shows the description, how the strategy works (or the world's guide and points table) and its actions. A newly made strategy starts folded out, and so does a world when it is the only one.
- **Sound (films only):** off until the viewer presses a film's Sound button; one setting for all films, remembered in the browser (`components/sound.js`). Effects and music are square and triangle waves made in the browser, no files. The voice is one recorded clip per caption under `public/audio/voice/en/`, listed in `manifest.json` (`{"clips": {"<clip name>": "<file>"}}`); the clip name is a hash of the caption's exact text, so a changed caption simply has no voice until it is recorded again. `/how-it-works?voice-script` lists every caption with its clip name. A spoken caption holds the film until the clip ends; pausing the film pauses the voice and the music. A film must stay fully understandable with sound off.

## Motion

When a result is first shown, the race plays as a film of about 30 seconds (user, 2026-11-22), match day by match day: the fixtures appear at 0–0, their scores tally up while the lines move, then a roll-call goes through the competitors in their new order, one at a time (that line white and thick, its end label inverted, the rest dimmed, and a caption in display type with place, name, points and how it moved: "takes the lead", "up 2", "holds"), and the fixtures are wiped. A match day takes between 2.8 and 9 seconds, so a season of more than ten match days runs longer than 30 seconds; from nine match days on, only the top three are named. Afterwards, pointing at a line or its end label makes that line white and thick and dims the rest, so its path can be followed; and picking a match day, by clicking its column in the chart or its numbered button under the chart, lights that column and brings back the fixtures board with that day's final scores and a Close button. A match picked from the head-to-head table fills in its rounds one at a time (about 70ms each) while its scores count up.

- Provide a Replay control.
- Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, show the finished screen with no animation. Landing-page films then show each chapter as its finished picture with its last caption, one step at a time; the hero scene shows its finished picture.
- Play the full sequence when a result is first shown; do not replay it on every navigation back to the same result.

## Accessibility

- All text meets 4.5:1 on black (`--muted` is about 8:1). Do not use `--ghost` for text.
- Colour must never be the only cue: large move squares carry their letter, the legend is always visible, and tables keep the text label too.
- Use real `<a>`, `<button>`, `<table>` (for the leaderboard and matrix) and headings; decorative blocks get `aria-hidden="true"`.
- Layout must work at phone width with no sideways scrolling anywhere (user, 2026-11-22): the header wraps, columns stack, strips wrap onto further lines, the head-to-head table shrinks to fit, and wide tables (`.grid-table.stacks`) turn each row into a block of labelled lines.
